Abstract

Diffusion language models predict tokens in parallel but still require many iterative forward passes. Adaptive sampling accelerates local token commitment, while recent global stopping methods seek to end generation early. Our trajectory analysis shows that short answers can stabilize early in decoding, whereas reasoning outputs often require more steps to settle. A fixed progress threshold therefore struggles to avoid both redundant computation on short-answer tasks and premature termination on reasoning tasks. We introduce C4, a training-free framework that combines local decoding acceleration with adaptive global early exit. Confidence-Verified Early Exit (CVEE) determines when to end generation, skipping the remaining steps once its stopping criterion is met. It combines confidence and temporal agreement to adapt stopping to the observed revision history. Commit-Core-Then-Confirm (CCTC) accelerates token commitment while generation continues, reducing the decoding steps needed to complete each block. It extends confidence-threshold parallel decoding with a boundary-anchored core and one-step confirmation of deferred predictions, constraining additional commitments to mitigate premature freezing under unresolved context. With CVEE calibrated once on LLaDA, the same numerical settings transfer across all 12 zero-shot tasks and to Dream without recalibration. Under this configuration, C4 reduces decoding steps by 64–95% and achieves measured decoding-loop speedups of 2.6×–18.6×, with task performance close to full decoding. Component analyses show complementary contributions: early exit supplies most savings on short-answer tasks, while local acceleration contributes more on reasoning and code generation.

Method

Before C4

Why earlier methods fall short.

Diffusion decoding can be sped up in two places: commit tokens faster inside a block, or stop the whole sequence early. Earlier methods do one or the other, and each breaks somewhere (LLaDA-8B, paper Tables 1–2).

Stop early · Prophet, SchED

They stop on a schedule or a fixed confidence rule, not when the answer has settled, so reasoning is cut off while the answer is still changing.

Observation

The answer settles at very different times.

A diffusion LM shows its full provisional output at every step, so the current answer can be read long before decoding ends. On full LLaDA decodes, the point by which 90% of answers have stopped changing ranges from 4% of the schedule on short-answer tasks to 96% on long reasoning. No fixed stopping point fits both, so C4 stops on evidence from the answer itself and gets its speed on long outputs from faster commitment inside each block.

How C4 works

Commit locally, exit globally.

CCTC · which tokens to commit now

In the active block, confident positions next to the resolved boundary form a core and commit at once; the core may step over one unresolved position (H = 1). Confident positions further right are deferred and commit at the next step only if they still predict the same token. The decoder's own scheduled commits are kept, and confirmation reuses the next forward pass, so nothing extra is computed.

CVEE · when to stop the sequence

At every step a fixed parser reads the current answer. Decoding stops once

ct ≥ 0.7 mean confidence over the answer
runt ≥ max(3, ⌈2 · changest⌉) steps unchanged; more after each revision

The rule never looks at how far decoding has progressed; the remaining masks are filled in one step.

Results

12 zero-shot tasks: five short-answer (64-token output), five long-reasoning (256) and two code tasks graded by hidden tests (384), on LLaDA-8B-Instruct and Dream-7B-Instruct. Speedup is tokens per second relative to full decoding of the same model, on one RTX 4090.

Where the savings come from

Early exit carries short answers; faster commitment carries reasoning and code.

From full decoding, add the CVEE exit gate, then the CCTC core, then confirmation, then CCTC on every block (= C4). Bars are average decoding steps (paper Table 3).

Confidence alone stops a 384-step HumanEval program after 9.7 steps: accuracy 40.2 → 4.9.
Stability alone stops GSM8K after 24.5 of 256 steps: accuracy 66.0 → 33.0.

Requiring both keeps full-decoding GSM8K accuracy on both backbones but waits almost the whole schedule, which is why the reasoning speedup has to come from CCTC.

Related work

C4 builds on and compares with these works on diffusion language models and their acceleration.

  • [ICLR 2026] Fast-dLLM: Training-Free Acceleration of Diffusion LLM by Enabling KV Cache and Parallel Decoding

    Confidence-threshold parallel decoding; CCTC extends its commit rule with a core and one-step confirmation.

  • [ICLR 2026] Accelerating Diffusion Large Language Models with SlowFast Sampling: The Three Golden Principles

    Commits tokens faster from token-level statistics; a local-sampling baseline.

  • [NeurIPS 2025] KLASS: KL-Guided Fast Inference in Masked Diffusion Models

    KL-guided commitment of stable tokens; a local-sampling baseline.

  • [ICLR 2026] Wide-In, Narrow-Out: Revokable Decoding for Efficient and Effective DLLMs

    WINO: commits broadly and re-masks disputed positions; the sampler we pair with CVEE in the composition study.

  • [ICLR 2026] Diffusion Language Model Knows the Answer Before It Decodes

    Prophet: stops when confidence over a fixed region clears staged thresholds; the main early-exit baseline.

  • [ACL Findings 2026] Fast-Decoding Diffusion Language Models via Progress-Aware Confidence Schedules

    SchED: stops when confidence clears a schedule that depends on decoding progress; an early-exit baseline.

  • [ICML 2026] Efficient-DLM: From Autoregressive to Diffusion Language Models, and Beyond in Speed

    Its study of decoding order motivates CCTC's boundary-anchored core.

  • [ECCV 2026] DiffusionVL: Translating Any Autoregressive Models into Diffusion Vision Language Models

    Blockwise prediction that also informs the core.

  • [NeurIPS 2025] Large Language Diffusion Models

    LLaDA-8B-Instruct, the backbone C4 is calibrated on.

  • [arXiv 2025] Dream 7B: Diffusion Large Language Models

    Dream-7B-Instruct, the second backbone, used with the same frozen setting.
